## 1. Non-vacuity / role-preservation rule

CORE5 must not be made unfalsifiable by treating `Omega` as an arbitrary container for every missing object.

A reduction is legal only when semantic roles remain recognizable.

### Candidate `Omega`

Represents what may be the case / the realization being inferred. It may be structured or meta-level when the target itself is a structure, but it must not contain the observed answer or task verdict merely to force sufficiency.

### Observation `Psi`

Represents the accessible readout under the protocol. It must not be replaced by hidden-state information unavailable to the observer.

### Compatibility `K`

Represents consistency between predicted/readout information and the reported observation. Contract-specific enrichments may preserve compatibility witnesses, but `K` must not be used as an arbitrary storage field for task results.

### Task observables `O_T`

Represent distinctions that matter for the declared task. They must not redefine the underlying candidate merely to manufacture decidability.

### Dynamics / transport `delta`

Represents admissible evolution, update or transport. It must not be an arbitrary lookup table encoding the desired answer.

### Contract `c`

Types the above choices and declares legal gauge/reduction. It cannot hide the conclusion as a hypothesis.

This rule makes the R4 test nontrivial.

---

## 2. R4 admission criterion

A sixth primitive may be proposed only if all of the following are exhibited:

1. a frozen task and contract;
2. a concrete task-relevant distinction;
3. failure of every role-preserving encoding through candidate, observation, compatibility, task observables and dynamics/transport;
4. failure of legal contract/gauge enrichment;
5. a typed information-loss witness;
6. a proposed new role with a semantic function not equivalent to any existing role;
7. minimality: removing the new role restores the loss.

Symbolically:

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{FAIL}_{\Omega}
\land
\mathrm{FAIL}_{\Psi}
\land
\mathrm{FAIL}_{\mathcal K}
\land
\mathrm{FAIL}_{\mathscr O}
\land
\mathrm{FAIL}_{\delta}
\land
\mathrm{FAIL}_{c}
\land
\mathrm{LOSS}
\land
\mathrm{MINIMALITY}
\Rightarrow
\mathrm{R4\ candidate}.
}
\]

Anything weaker is a representation, typing, domain or documentation problem.

## 6. Evidence D — HIGHER-FIBRE

### Pressure

For

\[
*\to B\mathbb Z_2\leftarrow *,
\]

a strict object-set pullback is one point while the weak pullback has two compatibility witnesses.

Thus coarse truncation before fibre formation can destroy task-relevant data.

### Legal reduction

If witness/stabilizer information is task-relevant, the contract must preserve it in the structured candidate/compatibility representation before applying any coarse quotient.

A proposed quotient/truncation map is legal only if

\[
\ker q\subseteq E_{\mathcal T}.
\]

### Semantic caution

This branch exposes the strongest typing pressure of the four. A proposition-valued compatibility relation may be too coarse when multiplicity/coherence of compatibility witnesses matters.

The correct response is to enrich the **existing compatibility role** or candidate representation under an explicit contract, not to declare `higher fibre` a sixth semantic role merely because the mathematical type is richer.

### Verdict

\[
\boxed{\mathrm{HIGHER\ FIBRE}:\ NO\ R4.}
\]

What changed: legal type/representation of compatibility and gauge reduction.  
What did not change: semantic function `compatibility`.
